01 — The problem

Flood water arrives on a schedule nobody published.

A river rarely fails without warning. Rain falls, soil saturates, the catchment fills, and then — often a day or two later — discharge spikes. The signal exists. What is missing is a way to read it at stations nobody has built a bespoke model for.

FLOODCAST pulls gauge records, gridded weather and catchment surveys into one dataset, then learns the rainfall-to-discharge response well enough to transfer: a station the model has never seen still gets a near-term estimate.

“The lag between rainfall and peak discharge is where forecasting earns its value. Lead time is the product — a number without warning time is just a measurement.” Design principle — one-day horizon

03 — Model & approach

Built to survive a station it has never met.

A regressor is only useful if it transfers. Our validation splits by station, not by row — so the score reflects real deployment, not memorised gauges.

Feature engineering

Weather becomes memory: the model sees not just today's rain, but the rain that is still draining into the river.

  • Antecedent rain — 3, 7 and 14-day cumulative totals
  • Lagged signal — 1, 2 and 3-day discharge history
  • Catchment shape — area, mean elevation, slope, forest fraction
  • Seasonality — day-of-year sine/cosine pair

Gradient-boosting ensemble

Three sequentially boosted tree models, each trained on the same engineered features with different depth and learning-rate settings, blended into one prediction. Trees capture the non-linear, threshold-like behaviour of a rising river far better than a linear baseline.

Validation on unseen stations

Grouped split by station_id: no gauge appears in both train and test. A temporal hold-out on the latest season checks drift.

Extreme-event handling

Flood peaks live in the tail, where ordinary squared-error training underweights them. We train on log-transformed discharge, weight the top decile, and report peak-specific error separately.

  • log1p target to stabilise variance across basins
  • Tail weighting on events above the 90th percentile
  • Peak-timing check alongside magnitude error

06 — Limitations & future scope

What we have not solved yet.

A forecasting tool earns trust by being specific about its edges. These are ours.

Limitations

Honest boundaries of the current model build.

  • Weather is observed, not forecastThe model ingests measured rainfall — operational use needs forecast drivers and their uncertainty.
  • Snowmelt and reservoirs are unmodelledBasins dominated by storage or snow behave unlike the rain-driven catchments in training.
  • Single-day horizonLead time stops at 24 hours; multi-day ensemble forecasts are out of scope for this build.
  • Gauge record gapsMissing days are gap-filled, which smooths genuine short spikes in the training signal.

Future scope

Where this goes next, in priority order.

  • Forecast-driven inferenceSwap observed weather for Open-Meteo forecast APIs and propagate driver uncertainty.
  • Ordinal alert levelsConvert regression output into action-grade tiers with calibrated exceedance probabilities.
  • Confidence bandsQuantile regression to publish a P10–P90 envelope instead of a single line.
  • Regional expansionExtend beyond CONUS stations with per-region feature engineering and drift monitoring.
